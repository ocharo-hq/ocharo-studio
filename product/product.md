# Uzbek Studio — Product

*Last updated: 2026-09-25*

Uzbek Studio is a browser-based 3D creative studio for composing contemporary Uzbek clothing looks. A designer chooses an authored garment, applies textile images to its selectable parts, positions each pattern, and saves the result as an editable look.

## Product promise

Make textile-led garment exploration immediate and visual without requiring 3D, CAD, or sewing software expertise. Every control should answer a visible design question: which garment, which part, which textile, and where should its pattern sit?

## Primary user

The audience is designers and creative users exploring Uzbek clothing. Maftuna's confirmed v22 catalogue supplies the initial visual references; her role as a product user or validation partner has not been confirmed.

## Core experience

- A fixed mannequin presents one authored, single-size garment at a time.
- Five catalogue families establish the initial design language: `nimcha`, `baska`, `kostyum`, `korset`, and `abaya`.
- Each garment declares its own selectable parts and built-in attachments. Buttons, ties, and knots load with the garment and cannot be added freely.
- A material applies to the selected garment part with one action.
- Texture rotation and U/V offsets position the textile pattern on that part. Camera orbit remains a separate inspection action.
- The editor supports undo, naming, saving, reopening, and presentation export.
- The interface uses a central mannequin, floating cloud or island panels, a left menu, a contextual top toolbar, and large labelled icons or accessible tooltips.

## Product boundaries

The first release does not provide garment construction, arbitrary mesh editing, free placement of attachments, sizing, length changes, sewing patterns, pricing, ordering, or checkout. Decorative overlays and size or length controls are future possibilities, not first-release commitments.

Catalogue photographs guide new authored assets but do not prove hidden construction, dimensions, drape, or fit. The studio will not promise exact 3D replicas where the source lacks sufficient views. Source media, generated visual studies, and authored 3D assets retain separate provenance.

## Content foundation

The confirmed v22 catalogue contains 29 canonical products across the five families. Real source media grounds every canonical product; generated studies remain visibly distinct. No reusable 3D garment assets were found, so garment modelling and material-region authoring are required product inputs.

## Success criteria

A first-time user can choose a nimcha, select a declared part, apply and position a textile, inspect the look, save it, reopen it without losing editability, and export a preview without instruction from a 3D specialist. Subsequent garment families should enter through the same product contract without changing this creation loop.
